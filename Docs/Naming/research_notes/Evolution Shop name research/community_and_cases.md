# Game names: community views, comparable games, and rename case studies (for "Evolution Shop")

Scope note for the report writer: Reddit cannot be reached by this research tool (the crawler is blocked: "domains are not accessible to our user agent: ['reddit.com']"), and X, Bluesky and Discord servers can't be searched either. So the "community" view below comes mostly from marketers who summarise community data (Chris Zukowski of How To Market A Game, Simon Carless of GameDiscoverCo), itch.io devlogs and forums, and press interviews. Facts marked "(search snippet)" come from a search result summary, not from a page I opened, so they are weaker. Nothing here proposes a name.

## 1. What developers and marketers report about naming (data, experiments, regrets)

### Takeaway
The best data available says a name matters less for Steam search than devs fear: most store searches are for a name the player already heard elsewhere, so the name's real job is to be said, heard, remembered and typed correctly after a stream, a festival or a friend's tip. It also has to hint at the genre together with the capsule art. Keyword-stuffed titles are declining, and length doesn't predict sales. Generic real words and strange spellings are what devs regret.

### Cited Findings
**Chris Zukowski, "Are game names getting longer..." (How To Market A Game, 3 Feb 2025)**, the most data-based source found:
- He analysed 94,371 Steam games released 2016 to 2025. Average title length has not grown, and most titles are 1 to 3 words — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- Putting genre keywords after a colon (his example from Simon Carless: tinyBuild's "ALL WILL FALL: Physics-Based Survival City Builder") has declined in recent years. He calls keyword stuffing a tactic aimed at "low-information" mobile buyers that risks looking "cringe" on Steam — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- Title length doesn't correlate with sales. Six games since 2016 had one-character titles (1, 7, 8, b, S, ∞), and they didn't sell better for it — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- He thinks search optimisation matters little for naming: Steam and Google reliably find a game by its name plus "game steam". "So as long as you aren't naming your game a combination of emoji characters, you will be fine." The one real search risk he found was the game "1", which was renamed to a double emoji and became hard to find — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- Shoppers mostly find games through festivals, streamers, Reddit and social media, and search Google only after hearing about a game elsewhere — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- His sayability test: a title must be understood when shouted across a noisy show floor, and spelled correctly when typed in later. Bad examples he gives: "The revenge of ga't'ri'ju'zzs", "Mike Krzyzewski's Free-Throw Challenge", "Curse of the Legend of the Mystery of the Sages of the Spirits" — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- "YouTube viewers watch on phones but search on PCs", so creators must be able to say the name cleanly. Coverage by big creators shows up as Steam "Search Suggestions" and "Direct Traffic: google" — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- "Ironically, I think you don't want your title to be so unique that nobody knows what it is." Steam gives few places to signal genre, so capsule, title and tags must work together. In his guessing test, capsule plus title revealed the genre quickly. Titles need "enough genre hints" without becoming "cliché word salad", and there's no formula — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- About 13% of Steam titles use a colon on average. "Wholesome" and "Open World Survival Craft" games use one most (about 16%), and puzzle platformers least (8.4%) — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- He generated example names for each genre with ChatGPT and found many weak or comical. He said "Purrrfound" was not a real pun and joked that "Balling Harvest Snack Rocketfingers" sounded drunk — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)

**Simon Carless, GameDiscoverCo**
- "The majority of the time, people searching on these stores are typing in the name of a particular game." Example: "Type in Lethal Company, first result is… Lethal Company. Job done." Players who find a game on YouTube or Twitch then search for it by name — [GameDiscoverCo, "SEO on game platforms", 20 Mar 2024](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/seo-on-game-platforms-why-dont-we)
- "Title matches rank highest, and some devs use keywords in their title to improve SEO" (example: "Ambulance Life: A Paramedic Simulator"). But "Steam will also manually change SEO-y titles if they feel like they're deliberately extraneous" — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/seo-on-game-platforms-why-dont-we)
- Searches by subject ("truck driving game", "simulator") only help games that are already at least moderately popular. Otherwise a game shows far down the results unless the title matches exactly. Supermarket Simulator was #1 for "simulator" — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/seo-on-game-platforms-why-dont-we)
- By Carless's estimate, about 90% of Steam searches are by game name, and only a minority are by subject seen in autocomplete (search snippet) — [GameDiscoverCo, "Steam search suggestions & premium positioning"](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-search-suggestions-and-premium)
- GameDiscoverCo's title-word study, as reported by Gigazine (7 Sep 2023): it covered the 213 words that appear in more than 100 Steam titles. The best median lifetime revenue went to "HD", "remastered", "pro", "gold", "manager" and "tycoon", and the worst to "runner", "prologue" and "jump". In a garbled sentence, the article seems to say dark words such as "dead" and "black" beat casual words like "farm" and "little". Its own caveat: this is correlation, probably reflecting the kind of game, not the word — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230907-name-game-titles-steam); headline also at [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/what-do-the-names-of-bestselling-steam-games-have-in-common-words-like-manager-tycoon-remastered-and-hd) (article body could not be read)
- A 2026 GameDiscoverCo post reportedly cites GIES data: "Simulator is the most common title word on Steam, with 2,398 titles, or 1.9% of the total", and "Survivors", "Backrooms", "Idle" and "Cozy" are rising as title words (search snippet; I couldn't identify which post, so treat as unverified) — [GameDiscoverCo newsletter feed](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/feed)
- "Cozy" is the fastest-rising keyword in the capsule descriptions of successful Steam games over five years, up 675% (this is description text, not titles) (search snippet) — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/life-sim/the-cozy-game-boom-is-the-clearest-trend-on-steam-over-five-years-of-data)

**Devlogs and forums (regrets and mechanics)**
- The dev of Gunkid 99 renamed it from "99999" because a number-only, generic title competes with unrelated results and couldn't be found even with "game" added. They note that "Bleed" is weak alone but "Bleed game" works, since players know to add "game". Their advice: Google a name before committing — [itch.io devlog](https://yongjustyong.itch.io/gunkid99/devlog/48419/gunkid-99-previously-99999-name-change-and-updates)
- Same devlog: a Steam page is fairly easy to rename, but once a demo is out, you have to contact Steamworks support to rename the demo — [itch.io devlog](https://yongjustyong.itch.io/gunkid99/devlog/48419/gunkid-99-previously-99999-name-change-and-updates)
- On itch.io, renaming is self-service from the edit page, with automatic redirects — [itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/606515/how-to-change-name-of-game)
- In an itch.io naming thread, a commenter warned that a suggested portmanteau sounded too close to an existing title: check near-matches, not just exact ones (search snippet) — [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/5139134)
- A common reason for a forced rename is a clash with an existing brand or trademark: Ooblets (see section 3), and an itch.io dev who renamed to avoid a TV franchise (search snippet) — [itch.io devlog](https://itch.io/devlog/501935/game-name-changed-surprise-read-it.amp)
- Generic online naming guides repeat: keep it short, ideally under three words; say what the game is; make it memorable and original. These are low-evidence sources — [Felgo](https://blog.felgo.com/mobile-development-tips/cool-names-for-games-how-to-pick-the-best-title), [Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/best-practices-game-naming-why-it-matters), [Game Developer (sponsored)](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/how-to-name-a-game)

### Inferences
- For a small Steam game, the name is mostly a word-of-mouth tool, not a search tool. The tests that matter are: can a streamer say it once and have viewers type it right, and does "<name> game" bring it up first on Google and Steam?
- Genre hints in the name still help, because Steam gives few other places to show the genre. But a genre subtitle after a colon is now a minority practice (about 13%, slightly more for "Wholesome" games), and Steam may strip a subtitle it sees as stuffing.
- Two failure modes keep recurring: names too generic to find (a number, a common word like "Bleed") and names too hard to spell (apostrophes, odd letter repeats). A two-word name of common English words, like "Evolution Shop", risks the first; an invented word risks the second.

### Gaps
- No A/B tests or polls comparing two names for one game, and no capsule experiment where only the name changed, were found. These may exist in Reddit threads or Discord servers I couldn't reach.
- No source reports a wishlist count before and after a Steam rename. It's unclear whether Valve's process has changed. One Steam forum result said a released game's name can only change through Valve, but I couldn't confirm which post said it.
- The unverified "Simulator is the most common title word" figure, and a figure of "$70,000 median gross for X Simulator titles since mid-2023" seen in a search summary without a clear source, need a primary source before use.

## 2. What players say about names that draw them in or put them off

### Takeaway
I found little direct player testimony (Reddit was unreachable). The secondary evidence points one way: players reach a game by its name after hearing or seeing it, so names that are easy to say, unambiguous to spell, and distinctive enough to be the first result spread best. A name that is itself a joke (Untitled Goose Game) can become part of the meme.

### Cited Findings
- Streamers and YouTubers: viewers hear the name on a phone and type it on a PC later, so it has to survive being said aloud once — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- The "shouted across a show floor" test, and typing it correctly later — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- A name players had to look up: Eric Barone dropped "Sprout Valley" because "the word Sprout was not pleasant to say" (search snippet of his Discord answer) — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/stardew-valley-creator-says-the-games-name-just-came-to-me-after-the-original-was-not-pleasant-to-say)
- A memorable odd name: a CNN story headline on Untitled Goose Game's reveal said people were "losing their honking minds" over a game "called 'Untitled Goose Game'" (search result title, via a syndicated copy) — [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2019/08/30/entertainment/honking-goose-game-trnd); [syndicated copy](https://www.southgatv.com/?p=20894)
- By release, one outlet argued "everyone knows it as 'Untitled Goose Game'" (search snippet) — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=342166)
- Vampire Survivors: fans joke that the game has no real vampires among its enemies. The name became a genre label ("survivors-like"), and "Survivors" is reportedly rising as a title word (search snippets from wiki mirrors and the unverified GameDiscoverCo figure above) — [Wikiwand: Vampire Survivors](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Vampire_Survivors)
- Players add "game" to an ambiguous title when searching, so a common word can still be found, with effort — [itch.io devlog](https://yongjustyong.itch.io/gunkid99/devlog/48419/gunkid-99-previously-99999-name-change-and-updates)

### Inferences
- In chat, short names win. Players and streamers shorten long names, as with Rocket League's 2008 predecessor, known as SARPBC. A name of more than two words, or one with a colon, will be shortened by its community, so the short form needs to be the distinctive part.
- A name with an obvious creature or object noun (goose, slime, potion) gives viewers something to type even if they mishear the rest.

### Gaps
- No direct player polls on "names that put me off" were reachable (r/cozygamers, r/Steam, r/IndieGaming). If the report needs player voice, someone should read those subreddits by hand.
- I found no source on how cosy-game players specifically react to "Simulator" in titles, or to invented words.

## 3. Case studies: renames and title choices with known outcomes

### Takeaway
The best-documented renames were caused by sound or trademark problems, not data: Sprout Valley to Stardew Valley (not pleasant to say; sounded like a Harvest Moon place), Moblets to Ooblets (trademark), and the 2008 SARPBC to Rocket League (a short, chantable name for the sequel). Several famous names were accidents or placeholders (Untitled Goose Game, Vampire Survivors). For most hits (Lethal Company, Dredge, Hollow Knight, Spiritfarer, Slime Rancher, PowerWash Simulator), no source explains the name choice.

### Cited Findings
**Stardew Valley (2016, ConcernedApe)**
- It was first called Sprout Valley. Barone said on the community Discord: "The game used to be called Sprout Valley... but the word Sprout was not pleasant to say," and the new name "just came to me" (search snippet, headline confirmed) — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/stardew-valley-creator-says-the-games-name-just-came-to-me-after-the-original-was-not-pleasant-to-say)
- The game's wiki adds that he felt "Sprout Valley" sounded like a Harvest Moon location rather than its own thing, and that he considered "Stardrop Valley" (search snippet) — [Gaming Bible](https://gamingbible.com/news/platform/stardew-valley-fans-learn-original-name-002410-20240213); also [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/stardew-valley-was-originally-called-sprout-valley-plus-other-development-changes/)
- At the 10th anniversary (Feb 2026), Barone posted prototype footage and called the Sprout Valley name "horrible" (search snippet) — [Omelete](https://www.omelete.com.br/games/criador-de-stardew-valley-mostra-primeira-versao-do-jogo)
- Lesson: the new name is a place name built from an invented word ("Stardew") that sounds soft and specific, and it moved away from a name that echoed the genre leader.

**Rocket League (2015) and Supersonic Acrobatic Rocket-Powered Battle-Cars (2008, Psyonix)**
- In 2008, Psyonix founder Dave Hagewood said the long name was deliberate: "Part of the reason for the long name is that we wanted to let gamers know right off the bat... we are willing to take risks and break some rules." They had considered "SocCar", still used in the office, but he was "not sure it would work out too well in Europe" — [Gaming Nexus interview, 12 Oct 2008](https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/1994/Psyonix-Studios-Interview/)
- The 2008 game got mixed reviews and was not a commercial success, though it built a loyal fan base (search snippet) — [Wikipedia: Supersonic Acrobatic Rocket-Powered Battle-Cars](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_Acrobatic_Rocket-Powered_Battle-Cars)
- The sequel was renamed Rocket League in February 2014 (alpha 27 Feb 2014). The article's tongue-in-cheek list of reasons included the old title making headlines too long — [TheSixthAxis, 20 Feb 2014](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2014/02/20/supersonic-acrobatic-rocket-powered-battle-cars-sequel-gets-renamed-to-rocket-league/)
- Many web pages claim Psyonix blamed the long name for poor sales. They are low-quality SEO pages, and I found no primary source for that claim (see Gaps).

**Ooblets (Glumberland; full release 2022)**
- Renamed from "Moblets" on 5 Dec 2016 because "there were a few obstacles in the path of registering it as our own trademark". They weren't infringing anyone, but "figured we'd play it safe and come up with something else that would be easier" to register. "We were never that invested in the name Moblets to begin with", and it was picked before the game idea existed — [ooblets.com](https://ooblets.com/2016/12/new-name-same-game)
- The title is the creatures' name ("find wild ooblets (that's the name of the creatures)"), and the world is "Oob" — [Xbox Wire, 22 Feb 2017](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2017/02/22/ooblets-xbox-one-windows-10/)
- Writer Ben Wasser on naming: "Some pop out at us, sometimes they're just dumb puns" — [Game Pilgrim interview, 2018](https://gamepilgrim.com/2018/08/24/developer-interview-glumberland)
- Lesson: an invented, cute creature word can carry the whole brand, but check that it can be trademarked early. Ooblets had to rename after building an audience.

**Untitled Goose Game (2019, House House)**
- Asked about the title, a developer said it's "because we couldn't come up with a better name". Internally it was "goose game". When they uploaded festival footage, they added "Untitled" to show it wasn't final, and submitted it as "Untitled" to a Texas festival in 2017. They asked Twitter for suggestions but kept the working title (search snippets) — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2019/09/feature_how_a_joke_hatched_gamings_most_horrible_goose); [Nintendo Life, almost-used name](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2019/10/untitled_goose_game_developer_reveals_the_cheeky_name_they_almost_used); [DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/untitled-goose-game-was-almost-a-titled-goose-game/)
- Lesson: a plain, literal name (the animal plus "game") with a deadpan joke matched the game's comedy and became part of its meme.

**Vampire Survivors (2022, poncle)**
- It grew out of Luca Galante playing the mobile game Magic Survival. No source explains the title choice (search snippets) — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/vampire-survivors-creator-didnt-have-a-vision-when-he-started-making-the-game-that-allowed-him-to-quit-his-job); [Medium/SuperJump](https://medium.com/super-jump/vampire-survivors-a-study-of-an-indie-phenomenon-7e30dbbf07e5)
- Galante later used the brand's strength for a deckbuilder spin-off, Vampire Crawlers (search snippet) — [kruxor mirror of interview](https://api.kruxor.com/view/game_news/TZBHM/im-making-this-for-me-not-to-make-everyone-happy-vampire-survivors-creator-on-his-next-game)
- In June 2026 the game was renamed "Vampire Survivors – First Survivaton", "Survivaton" being poncle's label for its survivors-like projects, short for "survive a ton" (search snippets) — [Nintendo Life, June 2026](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2026/06/vampire-survivors-announces-switch-2-version-new-dlc-and-name-change); [Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/video-games/3956080/vampire-survivors-receives-new-name-new-expansion-this-summer-trailer/); [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/vampire-survivors/legacy-of-bloodmoon-expansion/)
- Lesson: a two-word name of a thing plus a verb-noun became the name of a genre ("survivors-like"). A name that sums up the core loop can be copied as a genre label.

**Potion Craft: Alchemist Simulator (2022, niceplay games)**
- It is also titled "Potion Craft: Alchemist Simulator" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potion_Craft)
- Its Gamescom 2021 listing describes it as an alchemist simulator where you handle tools and ingredients physically, the same words as the subtitle — [Indie Arena Booth](https://indiearenabooth.de/gamescom2021/games/show/3185)
- A Russian preview says the name makes clear you'll mostly make potions by hand (search snippet) — [StopGame](https://stopgame.ru/show/122566/potion_craft_alchemist_simulator_preview_po_ranney_versii)
- No source gives the developers' reason for the subtitle.
- Pattern: a short "noun + verb" main title (Potion Craft) carries the brand, and a genre subtitle after the colon carries the keyword.

**PowerWash Simulator (2022, FuturLab)**
- The idea came from powerwashing videos on Reddit, and partly from simulators "constantly" being in the Steam Top 10 (search snippet) — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/for-futurlabs-powerwash-simulator-success-is-just-the-start-of-the-journey)
- FuturLab described a seriousness scale for simulators running from "Goat" (Goat Simulator) to "Train" (Train Simulator) (search snippet) — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2021/06/04/futurlab-power-wash-simulator-game)
- No source explains the title choice. Pattern: a coined compound for the activity ("PowerWash") plus "Simulator".

**Lethal Company (2023, Zeekerss)**
- No source explains the title. Players are contract workers for "the Company" collecting scrap on abandoned moons (search snippet) — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20231027-269829/)
- GameDiscoverCo uses it as the example of a distinctive name that searching finds at once — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/seo-on-game-platforms-why-dont-we)

**Hollow Knight (2017, Team Cherry)**
- Its knight first appeared in "Hungry Knight", a 2013 Ludum Dare jam game. No source explains the final title (search snippets) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollow_Knight); [Game Informer, "The Making of Hollow Knight"](https://gameinformer.com/2018/10/15/the-making-of-hollow-knight)

**Spiritfarer (2020, Thunder Lotus), Dredge (2023, Black Salt Games), Slime Rancher (2017, Monomi Park)**
- I found no source explaining these names. Searches only turned up the studio names' origins: Thunder Lotus is "something epic, but something beautiful", and Black Salt comes from the studio's "SALT District" (search snippets) — [Pocket Tactics (Spiritfarer)](https://www.pockettactics.com/spiritfarer/interview); [Gaming.net (Black Salt)](https://www.gaming.net/black-salt-games-interview-series/)
- Slime Rancher: studio founded 2014 by Nick Popovich and Mike Thomas, ex-Three Rings (search snippet) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slime_Rancher)

### Inferences
- The two renames with clear reasons came from sound (Stardew: unpleasant word; sounded like a Harvest Moon place) and trademark (Ooblets). Both are cheap to check before launch: say the name aloud many times, and search trademark registers and Steam for near-matches.
- Literal-but-specific names did well: Slime Rancher, PowerWash Simulator, Untitled Goose Game, Potion Craft. Each names a concrete creature or object and an activity, so a viewer who hears it once knows what it is. Invented names worked when the word was cute and easy to say (Stardew, Ooblets).
- The "Sprout Valley sounded like Harvest Moon" lesson applies here: a name that sounds like a known genre leader's location or creature (for example, words close to Pokémon, Slime Rancher or Viva Piñata terms) may read as derivative.

### Gaps
- No primary source says the 2008 SARPBC name hurt sales. That claim circulates only on low-quality pages. The 2014 rename article gives humorous reasons.
- I didn't research the names of Dave the Diver and Unpacking (out of time). No reasons were found for Lethal Company, Dredge, Hollow Knight, Spiritfarer, Slime Rancher or PowerWash Simulator, so treat any story about them as unverified.
- No case study measured a name's effect on wishlists or sales. All the "outcomes" are the games' overall success, which the name can't be separated from.

## 4. The naming landscape of the closest comparable games

### Takeaway
The closest comparable games cluster into four patterns: (a) a literal creature or object noun plus a role or activity (Slime Rancher, Monster Sanctuary, Cassette Beasts, My Singing Monsters, Potion Craft, PowerWash Simulator); (b) an invented or portmanteau word (Ooblets, Equilinox, Megaquarium, Potionomics, Spiritfarer); (c) a place name (Stardew Valley, Moonstone Island); (d) a single real word (Spore, Seaman, Niche, Creatures, Dredge, Unpacking, Aquarist). The aquarium niche is crowded with literal names (Aquarist, Aquarium Designer, The Aquarium, Fish Game, Pond Keeper), which makes them hard to tell apart. The standouts pair a concrete, cute noun with a twist.

### Inferences
- **Literal noun + role (the most common pattern among hits):** Slime Rancher, Monster Sanctuary, My Singing Monsters, Cassette Beasts, Potion Craft. These pass Zukowski's genre-hint test. The creature or object word is the searchable part, and the second word gives the verb or setting.
- **Portmanteaus of genre words:** Megaquarium (mega + aquarium) and Potionomics (potion + economics) blend two real words, so they're spelled as heard and still show the genre. They seem the safest kind of wordplay.
- **The day/night double meaning:** Moonlighter (shopkeeper by day, adventurer by night, if memory serves) is a real word whose second meaning describes the loop. Evolution Shop has the same loop (sell by day, breed at night), so a name that hints at both halves follows a proven pattern. Flag only; this researcher doesn't propose names.
- **Creature name as title:** Ooblets and Ova Magica name the creatures or their world. The game's own vocabulary (families like Brookkin, Dewling, Cinderling, Reefback, Gloamer; cultures like Bloom, Ember, Brine; helpers Moss and Nori) uses soft English roots with -kin and -ling endings. That vocabulary is a ready source of a coined word in the same style.
- **Place names:** "Valley" and "Island" are common in cosy titles (Stardew Valley, Moonstone Island). A place name signals cosiness but adds little genre information, and these words are crowded.
- **Crowded and literal:** the aquarium niche (Aquarist, Aquarium Designer, The Aquarium, Fish Game) shows how generic real words blur together. "Evolution Shop" is two common English words and risks the same problem (see section 1). Neither word names the cute creature, the water, or the lab.
- **"Simulator":** a strong keyword on Steam (Supermarket Simulator #1 for "simulator"), but it reads as tongue-in-cheek or serious depending on the game (FuturLab's Goat to Train scale). As a subtitle after a colon it's still common in sims (Potion Craft precedent), but Zukowski finds colon stuffing declining and Steam may remove extraneous subtitles.

## 5. Do puns and wordplay help or hurt?

### Takeaway
I found no data. Practitioners' opinions agree: wordplay helps when it's an easily spelled blend of real words that still says what the game is (Potionomics, Megaquarium). It hurts when it depends on odd spelling or only works in writing (Zukowski's "Purrrfound", "sofishticated"), because that breaks being said aloud, typed after a stream, and translated.

### Cited Findings
- Zukowski called ChatGPT's "Purrrfound" "not a real pun" in his review of generated names — [howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/?p=4464)
- A title guide recommends alliteration or wordplay to stand out, but says a title should reflect the core gameplay or story (search snippet) — [Wala Wala Games](https://www.walawala.gg/games/make-your-indie-game-stand-out/)
- Critics mock weak puns ("you can't just mush two words together and call it wordplay"), and one notes a pun that only works when seen written down (search snippets) — [Zero Punctuation wiki, "A Rant About Video Game Puns"](https://zeropunctuation.fandom.com/wiki/A_Rant_About_Video_Game_Puns); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/video-game-title-puns/)
- A pun works when it is tied to the game: Crash Bandicoot 4: It's About Time refers both to the long wait and to its time-travel story (search snippet) — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/games-best-iconic-puns/)
- Ooblets' writer admits some of their names "are just dumb puns", a style that suits a light comedic game — [Game Pilgrim](https://gamepilgrim.com/2018/08/24/developer-interview-glumberland)
- Marketing guides put the store page and tags ahead of the title for discoverability (search snippet) — [Datahumble](https://datahumble.com/blog/indie-game-marketing-guide); [Immutable](https://www.immutable.com/guides/how-to-market-an-indie-game)

### Inferences
- For a pastel game with mild comedy, light wordplay fits the tone, as Ooblets shows. The safe form is a blend whose parts are real, correctly spelled words (as in Megaquarium, Potionomics), rather than a spelling twist (sofishticated).
- A pun that needs to be seen written will be lost when a streamer says it. A pun on English sounds may not survive translation, which matters if the game is localised.

### Gaps
- No study, A/B test, or post-mortem measured whether a pun title helped or hurt a game. All evidence is opinion.
- No source covered how pun titles fare in translation for any specific game.
