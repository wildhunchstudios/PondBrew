# Trademark, domain and handle checks for the shortlisted "Evolution Shop" titles

This is a preliminary search made on 2026-11-03, not legal clearance and not legal advice. It records what three public trademark databases, the .com registry and five social platforms showed on that day. A name that looks clear here can still collide with an unregistered (common-law) mark, a mark in a register these tools don't cover, an application filed after these searches, or a mark a trademark examiner would judge confusingly similar in a way a word search misses. A trademark lawyer's clearance search is the step that clears a name.

Names checked: the 13 on the shortlist, plus three the earlier notes mark as among the strongest or clearest but the shortlist left out: **Tanks & Tinctures** and **Brood & Breakfast** (named in the earlier notes' takeaway as strongest) and **Cometail** (listed there among the clearest coined compounds) ([candidate_names.md](file:///C:/dev/DumbVideoGameStudio/Docs/Naming/research_notes/Evolution%20Shop%20name%20research/candidate_names.md)).

## What is each name's preliminary verdict?

### Takeaway
Six names come out **clear**: Mistfin, Pondbrew, Gloamwater, Moss & Nori, Mix & Hatch and Tanks & Tinctures. Eight are **caution**: Brinewell, Tidebrew, Bubblebrook, Oddfin, Bloom & Brine, Bubbles & Brine, Brood & Breakfast and Cometail, mostly because the .com or most handles are taken, or a small business uses the exact name. Two are **conflict**: **Hatchpot** (a registered Japanese "Hatch-Pot VR" mark in class 9, a live visual-novel platform called Hatch-Pot Novel on hatchpot.com, and a tarot app using @hatchpot) and **Kelp Wanted** (one letter from live US "HELP WANTED" registrations for the Five Nights at Freddy's video games in classes 9 and 41). The strongest all-round results are **Moss & Nori** (no mark, mossandnori.com free, all five handles free) and **Pondbrew** (no mark, all three domains free, four of five handles free).

### Cited Findings

How the verdicts were set (my criteria, not a legal standard):
- **Clear**: no live or pending mark found with the same or a near-identical name, the main .com (or its "game"/"play" forms) free, and no active brand using the name; dormant personal accounts on some platforms are noted but don't change the verdict.
- **Caution**: no blocking mark found, but one or more of: the .com in active use or for sale, the exact name used by an active business or brand in another field, a near-identical mark in a related class, or matching handles taken on most platforms.
- **Conflict**: a live mark or active product in games, software or entertainment with the same or a confusingly similar name.

### Inferences
- The two conflicts are both puns that lean on a word or phrase someone else already owns in games: "Hatch-Pot" in Japan's class 9 register and a live visual-novel service, and "Help Wanted" in the US register for a famous horror game series. The more famous the other mark, the less similarity it takes to be a problem, so Kelp Wanted is the riskier of the two in the US and Hatchpot the riskier in Japan.
- The coined single words (Mistfin, Pondbrew, Gloamwater) and the two less obvious "X & Y" pairs (Moss & Nori, Tanks & Tinctures) are the cleanest: nothing in the trademark registers, free domains, and mostly free handles.
- "Caution" names are usable but would need a modified handle (for example adding "game" or "_game") and a domain such as name-game.com or playname.com, and would share search results with a small business or person.
- Mix & Hatch is clear today but made of ordinary words, which are harder to register and defend as a trademark than a coined word (this is general trademark practice, not checked against a source here).

### Gaps
- No register outside those covered by the USPTO, WIPO's Global Brand Database and TMview was searched; in particular Israel's own register, China's CNIPA, Korea's KIPO and Japan's J-PlatPat weren't searched directly (TMview does include Japan's JPO records, which is where Hatch-Pot VR appeared).
- Unregistered (common-law) use in the US and passing-off in the UK can't be found in registers; only the web and handle searches below speak to it.
- Pending applications filed in the last weeks may not yet appear in WIPO's and TMview's copies of national data.

### Inferences
- For Kelp Wanted, a player searching the title will likely be shown Five Nights at Freddy's: Help Wanted, a far larger game; that's a findability cost on top of the trademark risk.
- For the "& Brine" names, searches will share space with small food and drink businesses, not with games; this matters less for a game's store page than for its website and handles.
- The coined words with no web presence at all (Mistfin, Pondbrew, Gloamwater, Oddfin) would own their search results from launch, though Oddfin's handles are taken.

### Gaps
- Web searches were made for 7 of the 16 names (Hatchpot, Hatch-Pot, Kelp Wanted, Bloom & Brine, Bubbles & Brine, and the four coined words together); one combined search covered Mistfin, Oddfin, Pondbrew and Gloamwater, and the tool may have run it as a plain search rather than with OR, so a hit on one of them could have been missed. Tidebrew, Brinewell, Bubblebrook, Moss & Nori, Mix & Hatch, Tanks & Tinctures, Brood & Breakfast and Cometail had no web search of their own here (the earlier notes searched some of them).
- The size and reach of the small businesses and apps found (the Hatchpot tarot app, the Tide kava brand, Bloom & Brine, Bubbles and Brine) couldn't be measured from these sources.
