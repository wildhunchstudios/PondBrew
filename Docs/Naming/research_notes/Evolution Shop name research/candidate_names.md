# New titles for "Evolution Shop": candidates and their conflict checks

These are availability checks made from store and web searches on 2026-11-03, not legal clearance. A title that looks clear here can still collide with a registered trademark, an unreleased game, or a name in a store or country not searched. A trademark lawyer's search is the step that clears a name.

## Which name directions fit the game, and which candidates come from each?

### Takeaway
Seven directions give 40 usable candidates (listed under the next question with their checks): the game's own words, invented creature-like words, the shop as a place, puns, verb-led names, "X & Y" names, and plain descriptions of the lab. The strongest-looking directions, by how few conflicts they hit, are invented compounds built from the game's cultures and water words (Mistfin, Brinewell, Pondbrew, Tidebrew, Gloamwater), "X & Y" pairs of the game's own nouns (Bloom & Brine, Moss & Nori, Tanks & Tinctures), and a few puns (Hatchpot, Kelp Wanted, Brood & Breakfast). The weakest directions are names built on "aquarium", "tank", "pond", "critter" or "-lings", where the genre is already crowded.

### Inferences
- Names that say "aquarium", "fish shop", "tank" or "pond" will sit among dozens of near-identical shop simulators and war-tank games, so a distinctive coined word or a pun is the safer route to being findable.
- The game's creatures are invented, aquatic and mixed from coloured cultures, so the words that carry the game best are its culture names (Bloom, Flow, Ember, Spark, Mist, Brine, Dusk), water words (brine, tide, brook, puddle, kelp) and the verbs of the loop (mix, brew, dose, hatch, breed, sell).
- "Pondlet" itself, the game's early creature, now looks risky even inside the game, since "Pondlets" is a published mobile creature game and "PONDLINGS" a published PC pond game. This matters only if Pondlet is used in marketing or the title; it's worth a deliberate choice either way.

### Gaps
- No search was made of the console stores, the Epic Games Store, GOG, or Roblox, where more same-name games may exist.
- Steam's store search was read through two of its own search endpoints and its search page; games not yet announced, or with a store page hidden from search, can't be seen this way.

#### What was searched
- Steam: the store's own search for each name (its name search and its search page, counting games that match), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=Brood%20%26%20Breakfast) for example.
- itch.io: the site search, top results read for each name.
- App Store: Apple's public search for apps in the US store, top 10 results.
- Google Play: the US store's app search, top results; for three names the listing itself was opened.
- Web: 14 web searches covering 25 of the names (listed per name below); a "no web result" is noted only where a web search was made. Some web searches combined two or three names, and the search tool said it ran one of them as a plain search rather than with OR, so a combined search may miss a hit on one of its names.
- Trademark databases: the USPTO's trademark search (tmsearch.uspto.gov) and WIPO's Global Brand Database are JavaScript applications that returned no data to the tools here; the USPTO's search API refused a direct request (HTTP 405). EUIPO and the Israel Patent Office's trademark database were not tried.

#### Key to verdicts
- **Clear**: no game or app with this or a near-identical name found in the four stores, and no notable brand found where a web search was made.
- **Near**: no identical name, but a close neighbour (one letter off, the same root in the same genre, or a non-game product with the same name).
- **Rejected**: a game or app already uses the name, or the search is dominated by something else. These are in the second table.

### Gaps

- Steam's match counts for "Fizz & Fin", "Brew & Brood" and "Mix, Hatch, Sell" weren't obtained; its search stopped answering mid-run, and the third wasn't in the run. Steam's name search found no game with any of them.

## How does the current name, "Evolution Shop", fare?

### Takeaway
No game called "Evolution Shop" was found on Steam, itch.io, the App Store or Google Play, so it isn't taken. But it's buried: "evolution" is in 2,217 Steam games, including Frontier's "Jurassic World Evolution" series, a well-known board game and video game is called simply "Evolution", and on mobile "evolution" mostly means idle and merge games. It also describes the game poorly, since the creatures are mixed and bred, not evolved.

### Cited Findings
- Steam's name search finds no game called "Evolution Shop" — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=evolution%20shop)
- Steam's search page matches 140 games loosely for "evolution shop", led by "Internet Cafe Evolution", "Abyssal Evolution" and "Fusion Nexus: Alchemic Evolution" — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=evolution%20shop)
- "Evolution" alone matches 2,217 Steam games, among them "Jurassic World Evolution", "Jurassic World Evolution 2" and "Jurassic World Evolution 3", "Cell to Singularity - Evolution Never Ends", "Idle Evolution", "Slime Evolution" and "Goose Evolution" — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=evolution)
- itch.io's results for "Evolution Shop" are games called "Evolution" and unrelated shop games ("The Shopkeeper's Secret") — [itch.io](https://itch.io/search?q=Evolution%20Shop)
- A web search found no game titled "Evolution Shop"; its closest match was an unfinished jam entry on itch.io called "evolving shop" — [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/15845491/view-in-topic)
- The App Store's results for "Evolution Shop" include "My Card Shop Evolution Game 25", "TCG Card Shop Tycoon 2", "Monsters Evolution" and "Clone Evolution: Cyber War"; for "Evolution Store", "Pet Evolution Pet Store", "Idle Animal Evolution" and "Evolution Board Game" — [App Store](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=evolution%20shop&entity=software&country=us), [App Store](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=evolution%20store&entity=software&country=us)
- Google Play's top result for "Evolution Shop" is an app called "Evolution", followed by "Shop Titans: Crafting Tycoon" and "Aquarium Land — Fishbowl World" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=evolution%20shop&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)
- "Evolution" is a board game by North Star Games, sold widely ([Asmodee Canada](https://shop.asmodee.ca/en/nsg501)), with a video game version ([gg.deals](https://gg.deals/ca/game/evolution-the-video-game/)); the web search summary also said the digital version had over three million players and came to the Nintendo Switch, which wasn't checked against its source ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/?p=666432))

### Inferences
- With no exact match, "Evolution Shop" is available as far as these searches show, but a player searching for it would wade through thousands of "evolution" games, many from large studios.
- On mobile, "evolution" signals idle merge games, and in strategy it signals the "Evolution" board game; neither is this game.
- The game's creatures change by mixing doses and breeding pairs, not by evolving, so the name promises a different game.
- For a Hebrew speaker the word is easy, since Hebrew uses it too (evolutzya), and the English spelling is familiar.

### Gaps
- Trademarks containing EVOLUTION couldn't be searched; the word is common enough in game and entertainment names that some marks are very likely, but none was checked.

## Are the candidates easy to say and spell, including for a Hebrew speaker, and do any carry unfortunate meanings?

### Takeaway
Most candidates are short, plain English. The ones with spelling traps are Bubblewright ("wright"), Fin & Phial ("phial"), Gloamwater ("gloam"), Cometail (the shared t), Brookkin (the double k) and the puns that depend on an English phrase (Kelp Wanted, Brood & Breakfast, Odd Fry). A few words carry meanings that work against the game: "fizzle", "brood", "dose", "spawn", "wee", "backroom", "roe" and "tank".

### Cited Findings
- "Brood" also means to dwell on something worriedly — [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/brood)
- A search for "Backroom Hatchery" on Google Play returns "Backrooms" horror games — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=Backroom%20Hatchery&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)
- "Dosewell" is a medication reminder app, so "dose" names sit beside medicine — [App Store](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=Dosewell&entity=software&country=us)
- "Tank" on Steam is mostly war games: the top matches are "Tank", "SLAM TANK", "Tank Force: Online Shooter Game" and "Tank Battle: 1944" — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=tank)
- "Spawn" on Steam is a gaming term in thousands of games ("Spawn Kings", "Dead Spawn", "Dark Spawn: Infestation") — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=spawn)

### Inferences
- These judgements on pronunciation are mine, not from a source: Hebrew has no "th" sound, so "Something" in "Hatch Something Strange" tends to come out as "Samting"; silent letters ("wright") and "ph" for f ("phial") are hard to spell from hearing; most other candidates use sounds Hebrew has, including the "ch" of "hatch" (written צ׳ in Hebrew loanwords).
- None of the 40 kept names looked, to me, like a rude or unfortunate word in Hebrew; "Gill" in "Gill & Grow" is even a Hebrew word for joy and a common name. This wasn't checked with a native speaker.
- Puns that rest on an English phrase (Help Wanted, Bed & Breakfast, small fry) will miss some non-native players, though the name still reads as a plain phrase to them.
- "Wee" is British slang for urine and "Roe" is tied to Roe v. Wade in the US; both are from general knowledge and weren't checked against sources.

### Gaps
- No native Hebrew speaker or English speaker was asked to say the names aloud; a quick read-aloud test with a few people would settle the spelling and sound questions better than these judgements.
